# AWS Data Exchange — Categories

**URL**: https://aws.amazon.com/data-exchange/
**Scraped**: 2026-02-26
**Method**: WebFetch of main product page

## Category Taxonomy

### Industry / Domain Categories (Primary)
These are the main categories used to organize data products:

1. Advertising & Marketing
2. Automotive
3. CPG & Retail (Consumer Packaged Goods)
4. Financial Services
5. Gaming
6. Healthcare & Life Sciences
7. Manufacturing
8. Media & Entertainment
9. Public Sector
10. Telecommunications

### Solution / Use-Case Categories
AWS also organizes by business use case:

- Advertising & Marketing
- COVID-19
- CPG & Retail
- ESG & Sustainability
- Financial Services
- Healthcare
- Macroeconomics
- Media & Entertainment
- Technology
- Travel & Hospitality

### Data Delivery Format Categories
Products are also classified by delivery mechanism:

- **Data Files** — traditional file-based access via S3
- **Data Tables** — structured table format (Redshift Data Tables)
- **Data APIs** — REST API-based access (via API Gateway)
- **Data Managed in AWS Lake Formation** — governed data access via Lake Formation permissions

### Pricing Categories
- Free
- Paid (subscription, one-time, per-revision)

## Scale
- ~3,500+ data products
- 300+ data providers

## Notes

- Taxonomy is relatively **flat** — one level of industry categories, no deep sub-hierarchy
- Dual organization: by **industry vertical** and by **business use case** (with significant overlap)
- Delivery format is a separate, orthogonal classification axis
- Provider sets their own pricing; AWS takes a revenue share
- Integration with AWS ecosystem (S3, Redshift, Lake Formation, Athena, SageMaker) is the key differentiator
- The use-case categories (ESG, Macroeconomics, Travel) suggest AWS is thinking beyond pure industry verticals
